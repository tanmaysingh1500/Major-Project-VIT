# Public Charging Infrastructure Norms (Illustrative Summary)

Public EV charging in India generally spans a few broad categories:

- **AC slow/normal charging** — typically used for overnight or long-dwell
  charging (e.g. at home, workplaces, malls), delivering roughly single-digit
  to low-double-digit kW.
- **DC fast charging** — used along highways and at dedicated fast-charging
  hubs, delivering substantially higher power (tens of kW and up), aimed at
  reducing charging time during longer trips.
- **Charging connector standards** — India has seen a mix of connector types
  in use (e.g. CCS2 has become a common standard for many newer four-wheeler
  DC fast chargers), alongside older/other standards for two- and
  three-wheelers.

Government guidelines have generally encouraged:

- A minimum density of public charging stations along major highways
  (e.g. roughly every tens of kilometers) to support long-distance travel.
- Charging point availability in urban areas at a walkable/short-drive
  distance in dense localities.
- Standardization to reduce range anxiety and improve interoperability
  between charging network operators.

This summary is deliberately high-level and non-numeric where precise
figures would need verification against current official guidelines.
